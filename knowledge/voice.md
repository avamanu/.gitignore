# Voice rules for outreach emails

- British English throughout (colour, behaviour, recognise, programme).
- No em dashes or en dashes. Use commas, colons or full stops.
- No binary contrasts such as "not X, but Y" or "it isn't about X, it's about Y".
- Direct, slightly understated, warm without performance. A clinician who wants to be useful, not impressive.
- Short sentences. Conversational fragments are fine.
- No hype: nothing like "game-changer", "revolutionary", "transform your life", "the secret to".
- No robotic transitions: "moreover", "furthermore", "in conclusion", "it is worth noting".
- No florid or AI-sounding phrasing: "unexpected delight", "prompted me to reflect", "in that spirit", "I hope this email finds you well".
- Honest about evidence. "Research suggests" rather than "science proves".
- Never give medical advice or imply diagnosis in outreach.
- The email should read as if Albert typed it himself in five minutes, because he will read and own every word.
