# Voice rules for outreach emails

## Mechanics
- British English throughout (colour, behaviour, recognise, programme).
- No em dashes or en dashes. Use commas, colons or full stops.
- No binary contrasts such as "not X, but Y" or "it isn't about X, it's about Y".
- Short sentences. Conversational fragments are fine.
- The email should read as if Albert typed it himself in five minutes, because he will read and
  own every word.

## Tone
- Direct, slightly understated, warm without performance. Curious rather than certain.
- A clinician-adjacent scientist who wants to be useful, not impressive.
- He should not sound corporate, motivational, polished, or like a wellness guru.
- Occasionally provocative about ideas. Never provocative about people.

## Scientific language
- Real biology is welcome. Words like neuroinflammation, neuroplasticity, vascular injury and
  synapse are fine if the sentence explains them.
- Simplify the explanation. Do not simplify the reader. Assume an intelligent audience.

## Honesty about evidence
- "Research suggests", "evidence indicates", "one proposed mechanism is", "the study found an
  association". Never "science proves" or "scientists have proven".
- Distinguish association from causation. Never turn animal or cell research into claims about
  people, or into lifestyle advice.
- Never present preliminary findings as established.
- "The evidence is more complicated than that" is a sentence in his voice.

## Never
- No hype: "game-changer", "revolutionary", "transform your life", "the secret to".
- No clickbait claims: "cure dementia", "reverse ageing", "this changes everything",
  "doctors don't want you to know", "this one trick".
- No robotic transitions: "moreover", "furthermore", "in conclusion", "it is worth noting".
- No florid or AI-sounding phrasing: "unexpected delight", "prompted me to reflect",
  "in that spirit", "I hope this email finds you well".
- Never give medical advice or imply diagnosis.
- Never claim a credential he does not hold. He is not a doctor, neurologist, neurosurgeon or
  professor, and he does not have patients.
